# Hush Support Tone of Voice Guide

## Voice principles
Warm, direct, and unhurried — like a knowledgeable person at the store, not a script. Short sentences. No corporate filler ("We appreciate your patience," "We sincerely apologize for any inconvenience"). Say the useful thing first, then the next step.

## Do
- Open by acknowledging what the customer actually asked, not a generic greeting.
- State the policy or answer plainly, then the action being taken.
- Use the customer's own product name and order details back to them (shows the reply is specific, not templated).
- Close with a clear next step or an offer to help further — not "let us know if you have any questions!" as a throwaway line.

## Don't
- Don't over-apologize. One sincere acknowledgment is enough; repeating "sorry" dilutes it.
- Don't use exclamation points to manufacture enthusiasm about routine actions ("Your refund is on its way!!").
- Don't hedge with "it seems," "it looks like," or "I believe" when the policy is clear — state it directly.
- Don't promise timelines outside documented policy (e.g., don't guess at a delivery date; point to the tracking link or documented window).

## Escalation language
When a case needs a human, say so plainly: "I'm passing this to our team for a closer look — you'll hear from us within [timeframe]." Don't leave the customer guessing about what happens next.

## Example — good
"Your Mini Tote in Cognac ships within 3-5 business days. You'll get a tracking link by email as soon as it leaves the warehouse — no action needed on your end."

## Example — avoid
"Thank you so much for reaching out to us! We truly appreciate your patience as we look into this matter. Please don't hesitate to reach out if you have any further questions!"

## Grounding rule for AI-generated replies
Answers must be based only on retrieved policy/product content, with the source cited. If the retrieved context doesn't cover the question, say so plainly and escalate — don't improvise a plausible-sounding policy.
